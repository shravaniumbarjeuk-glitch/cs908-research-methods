# Research Methods: two written and spoken assignments

Two assignments from the MSc module CS908 Research Methods, University of Warwick, 2025-26. Individual work. No code: these show critical reading, argument and communication.

## Assignment 1: Digital twins for cardiovascular healthcare, a critical analysis

[`assignment-1-critical-analysis/Critical_Analysis_Digital_Twins.pdf`](assignment-1-critical-analysis/Critical_Analysis_Digital_Twins.pdf)

A five-page critical review of **15 papers (2023 to 2025)** on whether digital twins, virtual copies of a patient's heart or body, can work for real cardiovascular care.

- **Main argument:** a three-way conflict between data usefulness, speed and patient privacy that current systems struggle to satisfy together.
- Technologies such as blockchain and federated learning protect privacy but are slow for emergency use.
- The AI in these systems often acts as a black box, and until clinicians can understand its reasoning they cannot ethically trust it with patients' lives.
- **Conclusion:** future research should focus on making these systems safe and trustworthy, not only on making the models better.

## Assignment 2: Data That Never Dies, legal rights and the ethics of AI resurrection

[`assignment-2-presentation/Presentation_Slides.pdf`](assignment-2-presentation/Presentation_Slides.pdf)

A recorded talk (maximum 15 minutes) on an ethical, legal and social issue: what happens to a person's data after death, and whether AI should be used to simulate the dead. The 13 slides cover:

- **The scale:** the "digital graveyard" problem, with research projecting that dead users on Facebook could outnumber the living by 2070.
- **The legal gap:** digital assets were historically treated as non-transferable licences. The UK Property (Digital Assets etc) Act 2025 recognises digital files and crypto-tokens as personal property and lets executors manage digital estates. Platform policies (Apple's legacy contacts, Facebook's memorialisation) and jurisdictional friction with US privacy law still leave "orphaned data".
- **How "deadbots" work:** data ingestion, fine-tuning on a person's writing style, retrieval-augmented generation and voice and avatar synthesis, with a case study of the 2021 Project December simulation.
- **Ethical issues:** for the dead (consent, dignity, reputation) and for the living (frozen grief, dependency, the risk of a service shutting down).
- **A proposed framework:** a legal solution ("GDPR for the dead" and digital executors), a technical one (portable archive formats) and a design one (read-only memorial modes), plus a counter-argument on inheriting a digital inbox.

The video recording is not published (file size).
